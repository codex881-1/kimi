# Research and claim audit

## Editorial scope

The film concerns the **four documented Senior Bowl operational sorties** flown
against the Lop Nor nuclear test site between November 1969 and March 1971. It
does not treat allegations, sightings or later speculation as operational fact.
The route on screen is explicitly a schematic, not a recovered flight track.

A later memoir alleges that two additional sorties occurred and that one yielded
usable photographs. That claim is not corroborated by the operational histories
used here. The film therefore qualifies the count as four *documented* flights;
“zero film” refers to those four missions, not an absolute claim about every
alleged sortie.

## Claim table

| Claim in the film | Evidence and qualification |
|---|---|
| Lockheed built the D-21 for CIA reconnaissance; the Air Force conducted the operational launches | Declassified CIA interview, pp. 36–38; National Museum of the USAF fact sheet |
| The operational D-21B was carried under a B-52 and used a jettisoned booster | CIA interview, pp. 37–38; Goodall & Goodall, pp. 112–118 |
| It flew above Mach 3 at roughly 90,000 feet | National Museum of the USAF; CIA interview recalls altitude “up in the 90s” |
| The mission was pre-programmed and the payload was a high-resolution camera | National Museum of the USAF; Goodall & Goodall |
| The drone returned to a recovery area and released a film package for airborne recovery; the airframe was expendable | CIA interview, p. 37; Goodall & Goodall, p. 118 |
| Lop Nor in China was the target | Goodall & Goodall; Peebles, *Dark Eagles* |
| Mission 1, 9 Nov 1969: missed its turn and did not return | CIA interview, p. 37; Goodall & Goodall, p. 118 |
| Mission 2, 16 Dec 1970: reached the recovery area; partial parachute failure destroyed the recovery | Goodall & Goodall, p. 118 |
| Mission 3, 4 Mar 1971: parachute opened, airborne recovery failed, and the package was lost at sea | Goodall & Goodall, p. 118 |
| Mission 4, 20 Mar 1971: lost over China; wreckage was recovered by local authorities | Peebles, p. 131; subsequent museum documentation |
| The four documented missions produced no usable pictures | Goodall & Goodall; conventional operational history. See the additional-sortie caveat above |
| The program ended in 1971 | National Museum of the USAF and operational histories; exact cancellation date is not spoken |
| D-21B wreck no. 527 was later exhibited at the China Aviation Museum in Beijing | Archived Airforceworld account and 2010 photographic documentation |

## Date discrepancy

The second sortie is dated **16 December 1970** in the specialized Goodall &
Goodall operational history. Other web retellings use other December dates. The
film uses the directly cited operational-history date rather than repeating the
more common unsourced date.

1. **CIA FOIA, approved for release 29 December 2008.** *Interview with James A.
   Cunningham, Jr.*, 4 October 1983, CIA-RDP90B00170R000200240001-6,
   especially pp. 36–38. This is a participant recollection, so it is used for
   program mechanics and corroboration, not exact mission dates.
   https://web.archive.org/web/20170123104016/https://www.cia.gov/library/readingroom/docs/CIA-RDP90B00170R000200240001-6.pdf
2. **James C. Goodall and Nora D. Goodall (2002).** “Senior Bowl–the Lockheed
   D-21” [title spelling as printed in the citation], *International Air Power
   Review*, vol. 3, pp. 106–119, AIRtime Publishing. ISSN 1473-9917.
3. **Curtiss Peebles (1999).** *Dark Eagles: A History of Top Secret U.S.
   Aircraft Programs*, revised edition, Presidio Press, especially p. 131.
   https://archive.org/details/darkeagles00curt
4. **National Museum of the United States Air Force.** “Lockheed D-21B.”
   Technical and program fact sheet.
   https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Visit/Museum-Exhibits/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/195778/lockheed-d-21b/
5. **The Museum of Flight.** “Lockheed D-21B Drone.” Aircraft record.
   https://www.museumofflight.org/aircraft/lockheed-d-21b-drone/
6. **Airforceworld, archived 18 April 2019.** “Lockheed D-21B No. 527 now in
   the China Aviation Museum.” Used with photographic corroboration, not as a
   sole source for the wider operational history.
   https://web.archive.org/web/20190418091923/http://www.airforceworld.com/others/D21-mach-3-supersonic-reconnaissance-plane-crashed-in-china.html
7. **Public photographic record, 5 August 2010.** D-21 no. 527 wreck at the
   China Aviation Museum.
   https://www.flickr.com/photos/zaruka/4917830245/

No source supports a claim that China shot down any of these four drones, so the
film never makes one.
